# Secondary & Micronutrient Management: Zinc, Sulphur, Boron, Iron

## 1. Zinc (Zn) Management
- **Importance:** Crucial for chlorophyll synthesis, enzyme activation (carbonic anhydrase), and auxin (IAA) production.
- **Deficiency Symptoms:** "Khaira disease" in rice (bronze/rusty spots on younger leaves), white bud in maize, interveinal chlorosis in citrus.
- **Soil Application:** Zinc Sulphate Heptahydrate (ZnSO4 21% Zn) @ 25 kg/ha or Zinc Sulphate Monohydrate (33% Zn) @ 15 kg/ha as basal soil application.
- **Foliar Spray:** 0.5% Zinc Sulphate (5g ZnSO4 + 2.5g Slaked Lime per litre of water) at 30 and 45 days after sowing.

## 2. Sulphur (S) Management
- **Importance:** Essential for synthesis of amino acids (Methionine, Cysteine), protein synthesis, and oil content synthesis in oilseeds (Mustard, Groundnut, Soybean).
- **Deficiency Symptoms:** Uniform yellowing of younger leaves (unlike Nitrogen deficiency, which appears on older leaves first).
- **Application:** Elemental Sulphur (90% S) @ 25 kg/ha applied 2-3 weeks before sowing, or Agricultural Gypsum (18% S) @ 250 kg/ha, or Single Super Phosphate (11% S).

## 3. Boron (B) Management
- **Importance:** Essential for pollen grain germination, pollen tube elongation, flowering, and fruit set.
- **Deficiency Symptoms:** "Hollow heart" in groundnut, internal fruit browning/cracking in tomato and pomegranate, poor grain filling in sunflower and wheat.
- **Application:** Soil application of Borax (10.5% B) @ 10 kg/ha or foliar spray of Solubor (20% B) @ 1 g/litre during pre-flowering and fruit development stages.

## 4. Iron (Fe) Management
- **Deficiency Symptoms:** Interveinal chlorosis of youngest leaves turning into ivory white bleached leaves in high-pH calcareous soils.
- **Foliar Spray:** 1% Ferrous Sulphate (FeSO4) + 0.1% Citric acid sprayed twice at 10-day intervals.
