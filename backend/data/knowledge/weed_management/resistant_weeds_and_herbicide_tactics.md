# Managing Herbicide-Resistant Weeds & Modern Weed Control Tactics
*Authoritative Reference: AgricultureGuruji Agronomy Protocols*

## 1. Problem of Herbicide Resistance in Indian Cropping Systems
- **Major Problem Weeds:** *Phalaris minor* (Gullidanda) in wheat, *Echinochloa colona* in direct-seeded rice, *Parthenium hysterophorus* in fallows, and *Cyperus rotundus* (Motha) in cash crops.
- **Root Cause of Resistance:** Repeated mono-use of single chemical modes of action (e.g., continuous Isoproturon / Sulfosulfuron applications) creating target-site mutations.

## 2. Integrated Weed Management (IWM) Tactics
- **Mode of Action (MoA) Rotation:** Alternate between ALS inhibitors (Group 2), ACCase inhibitors (Group 1), and Photosystem II inhibitors (Group 5).
- **Stale Seedbed Technique:** Irrigate field 10-14 days prior to sowing to flush weed emergence, then eliminate seedlings via non-selective herbicide (Glyphosate or Glufosinate-ammonium) or shallow mechanical cultivation.
- **Critical Period of Weed Competition (CPWC):** Maintain weed-free condition for the first 25 to 45 days after sowing (DAS). Weeds emerging after CPWC do not significantly depress economic yield.

## 3. Crop-Specific Selective Herbicide Protocols
- **Rice (Transplanted):** Pretilachlor 50% EC @ 500 ml/acre within 0-3 days of transplanting with standing water.
- **Wheat:** Clodinafop-propargyl 15% WP @ 160 g/acre + Metsulfuron-methyl 20% WP @ 8 g/acre at 30-35 DAS.
- **Soybean / Groundnut:** Imazethapyr 10% SL @ 300 ml/acre at 15-20 DAS for mixed broadleaf and grassy weed knockdown.
- **Cotton / Maize:** Pendimethalin 38.7% CS @ 700 ml/acre pre-emergence followed by intercultural power weeding at 25 DAS.
